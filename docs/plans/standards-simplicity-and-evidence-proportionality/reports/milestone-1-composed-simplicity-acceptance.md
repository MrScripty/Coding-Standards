# Milestone 1 Composed Simplicity Acceptance

## Accepted Result

Milestone 1 makes simplicity an artifact-level property of independent
reasoning and change, not a synonym for familiarity, decomposition, test
volume, or construct count. Core now distinguishes simple from easy and names
complection directly. Architecture owns the composed-design admission probe,
including caller and composition-root knowledge, representative-change
Locality, Module Depth and Leverage, independent evolution, necessary
complexity, and deletion.

Planning requires every governed nonterminal plan to record whether that probe
applies. An applicable plan answers all eight artifact questions; an exclusion
records a concrete reason. A material replacement, unexpected propagation, or
cumulative machinery reruns the decision rather than inheriting the previous
review. Historical terminal plans remain exempt.

The codebase-design vocabulary used in these standards has a narrow role:
Module, Interface, Depth, Leverage, Seam, Adapter, Locality, and the deletion
test make the review operational. A named Module or Seam is not itself evidence
of simplicity, and a hypothetical Adapter may probe only a possible Seam's
shape. Permanent generality still requires a current independent reason.
