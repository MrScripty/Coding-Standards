User supplied definition
Discovery is substantial when resolving a material decision requires developing and revising a shared understanding across multiple questions, alternatives, or constraints. A decision is material when its answer could change the intended behavior, design direction, scope, or acceptance criteria.

Assess this as the discussion develops. When answers materially change other open questions or earlier design choices, use the substantial-discovery procedure.

Applicability examples supplied by the user
- A difficult API question may take considerable research but leave an otherwise understood design intact.
- A small feature can require substantial discovery when its behavior depends on unsettled questions about ownership, permissions, and recovery.
- A large implementation can need little discovery when its requirements and design are already well understood.
The distinction is between getting a missing answer and working out how several answers fit together. The definition is based on developing shared understanding, not task size, implementation difficulty, or a prior choice to produce a visual.

Required review scenarios
1. Small clear repair: ordinary implementation with inline write set and acceptance path; no forced discovery artifact or written plan.
2. Suggested mechanism masks another need: establish the underlying outcome while preserving a firm explicit choice.
3. Difficult isolated API question: bounded authoritative research; no automatic substantial-discovery classification from research duration.
4. Small feature with coupled ownership, permissions and recovery questions: substantial discovery with repeated reconciliation, concurrent independent research when available, and one separate visual writer.
5. Large implementation with settled requirements and design: apply written-plan sequencing criteria without manufacturing discovery.
6. Answers change other questions or earlier design choices: assess substantial discovery as the conversation evolves.
7. Research challenges an agreed decision or reveals an unrequested guarantee: discuss the consequence before representing it as agreed.
8. Direction changes: reconcile stale research and visibly retire stale alternatives in the visual.
9. Agents or local HTML are unavailable: report the actual limitation and use the specified bounded fallback.
10. Read-only parallel research: retain one synthesis owner without selecting concurrent-plan-integration merely from participation.
11. Provisional notes, visual or candidate design: cannot authorize implementation or acquire Planned status solely by being written.
12. Discovery-only Planning Prompt invocation: plan-specific steps remain conditional.
13. Discovery selected through a detail alone: the required Planning closure is selected even if routing.activities does not include planning.
14. Small ambiguous implementation request: the Implementation entry path reaches discovery.
15. Settled planning and ordinary uncertainty reduction: discovery is not automatically selected.
16. Development boundaries: explain coherent outcomes and evidence while preserving Commit's mechanics and no fixed branch or commit cadence.
